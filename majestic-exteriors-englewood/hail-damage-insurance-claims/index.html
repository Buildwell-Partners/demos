Your rights as a Colorado homeowner

  • Written contract required. Colorado law requires roofing contractors to give you a written contract with the scope, cost and timeline.
  • 72-hour cancellation. If your insurer denies the claim, you can cancel the roofing contract within 72 hours of the denial.
  • No deductible games. A roofer cannot pay, waive or rebate your deductible (C.R.S. 10-4-110.8). Walk away from anyone who offers.

FAQ

Hail and storm questions

How do I know if hail damaged my roof?

You usually can't tell from the ground. Look for dents in gutters, downspouts, window screens and metal vents, and for shingle granules collecting at downspout outlets. Hail about 1 inch across (quarter size) or larger often bruises asphalt shingles. Majestic Exteriors inspects roofs for free and photographs any damage so you can decide whether to file a claim.

Will homeowners insurance pay for a new roof after hail in Colorado?

If a covered storm damaged the roof, most Colorado homeowners policies pay to repair or replace it, minus your deductible. Older or cosmetic-damage exclusions vary by policy, so check yours. Majestic Exteriors documents the damage, meets your adjuster on the roof, and helps with the paperwork until the claim is settled.

Can a roofer pay or waive my insurance deductible in Colorado?

No. Colorado law (C.R.S. 10-4-110.8) bars roofing contractors from paying, waiving or rebating a homeowner's insurance deductible, and a contractor who offers to is breaking the law. Majestic Exteriors bills your deductible as required.

What is a Class 4 impact-resistant roof?

Class 4 is the highest rating under the UL 2218 impact test, in which steel balls up to 2 inches are dropped on the roofing. Class 4 shingles hold up better to Colorado hail, and some insurers offer a premium discount for them. Ask your insurer whether yours does.
